# Methodology — TalkGo

## Purpose
Develop the smallest functional communicative competence that is enough to understand, be understood and act with autonomy in real situations. Do not confuse essential communication with memorizing phrases or promising fluency.

## Principles
1. Adaptive diagnostic by skill: listening, speaking, interaction, general reading and technical reading when relevant; provisional CEFR with evidence and limitations. Technical knowledge can exceed oral skill.
2. Preparation before the conversation: 3–5 lexical items in context, up to two useful verb structures in the target language and 1–2 action expressions, with meaning and pronunciation suited to the variety. Do not impose English tenses on other languages.
3. Authentic simulation: one question at a time; characters, decisions, interruptions and unexpected events; communicative success first. Feedback afterwards, without constant correction.
4. Continuity: stable IDs, global numbering of phrases (F) and dialogues (D) per language, never reused, verified checkpoints and independent progress per language; voice and text keep evidence and limitations.
5. Functional and sustainable goals: missions with a target date set by the learner and practices of 30 minutes by default (at most 35), customizable; review every 14 days per language, or earlier if the target date is close.

## Default regular session
Every regular practice has exactly seven stages, announced to the learner, inside one length of 30 minutes by default and never more than 35 (the Entertainment Lab included): 1 Mission Briefing (3 min); 2 guided drill/review (4 min); 3 simulation (7 min); 4 unexpected event (4 min); 5 feedback (2 min); 6 closing: recap, phrases and next step (1 min); 7 Entertainment Lab (9 min), with a YouTube video linked to the practice content, chosen and verified when the practice is created, whose whole real duration is at most 25% of the planned length (`max_video_seconds = practice_minutes × 60 × 0.25`: 7 min 30 s in 30 minutes), with a comprehension activity. The Lab is part of the practice and is done before its completion: the practice is completed only after stage 7, when Finish-Practice finds the seven stages completed with evidence. Each stage has a saved state (pending, in progress, completed) and is completed only with evidence of execution; presenting a stage is not executing it. The learner can pause, repeat, review or ask for explanations without losing progress; a pause never becomes a completion. Adjust the times to the chosen length in the same proportion without dropping any stage. In the first practice, never claim a review of a practice that does not exist.

## Language adaptation
The skill sets the communicative goal; `targets/<language>/notes.md` and `essentials.md` set variety, forms of address, grammar, pronunciation, examples and situations. Explanations in the learner's native language. Never translate English phrases mechanically into other languages. Keep sociocultural differences and professional registers.

## Assessment and limits
Score comprehension, fluency, interaction, vocabulary and clarity from 0–5 only with evidence; otherwise "not assessed". Never interrupt the learner while answering: correct afterwards, objectively, with natural phrases, and give the dialogues in writing for visual review. Pronunciation cannot be inferred reliably from a transcript. Actual duration only if measured or confirmed by the learner's estimate; never invent it. Never create fictitious sessions, CEFR levels or imaginary transcripts.

## Immersion and curation
Entertainment Lab on YouTube as stage 7, transcript first: verify URL, real duration (never unknown or above the 25% limit), channel when shown, captions and actual access to the text; never invent transcripts or timestamps; respect copyright. Fit the curation to the selected language and variety.
