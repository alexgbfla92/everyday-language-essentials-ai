# Notes — Arabic (Modern Standard) (ar)

Explanations in the learner's native language. Keep diagnostics, practices, preferences and progress independent from the other languages. Use Modern Standard Arabic (فصحى) by default, written right to left in Arabic script with short vowels (harakat) up to A2. Up to A2, and whenever the learner asks, write every Arabic sentence with a romanized transliteration under it; let the learner answer in transliteration or Arabic script. Ask early whether the learner needs a spoken dialect (Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, Maghrebi): MSA serves reading, news and formal speech, while everyday conversation in each country uses a dialect; offer dialect phrases for the scenario when relevant and say which dialect they are. Focus on the root-and-pattern system, gender agreement, the definite article ال and sun/moon letters, verb-subject order in formal style, emphatic and pharyngeal consonants and politeness formulas. Never claim to assess pronunciation from a text transcript. Use present, past and future when relevant to the actual scenario, not as mandatory grammar coverage; one question at a time.
